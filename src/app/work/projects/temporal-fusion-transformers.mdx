## Overview

This project investigates the application of the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)—a deep learning architecture designed for multi-horizon time series forecasting—to the task of market regime detection. The goal was to determine if the TFT’s attention mechanisms could outperform traditional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models and unsupervised methods like Hidden Markov Models (HMM) in identifying structural shifts in financial markets.


## Methodology


1. Data Integration: The study utilized a diverse dataset (2000–2021) combining high-frequency daily price data for the SPY ETF with lower-frequency macroeconomic indicators, such as the VIX, Federal Funds Rate, and Economic Policy Uncertainty indices.


2. Feature Engineering: Key features included realized volatility (RV), RV lags, logarithmic returns, and squared log returns to provide the models with deep statistical context.



3. Regime Labeling: Because market regimes are not pre-labeled, an unsupervised K-means clustering technique was used to identify four distinct market regimes based on realized volatility.



4. Model Training: A TFT model (implemented via PyTorch Forecasting) was trained against a benchmark LSTM, both restricted to ~70,000 parameters to ensure a fair comparison of architectural performance.


## Key Findings
- Predictive Accuracy: The LSTM achieved a higher raw accuracy (86%) compared to the TFT (17%).

- The "Safe Prediction" Trap: Analysis revealed that the LSTM’s high accuracy was likely due to "safe" predictions—consistently outputting the most prevalent regime.

- Structural Insight: While the TFT had lower accuracy, it achieved a superior F1 score (0.92 vs. 0.14 for the LSTM), indicating a much better balance between precision and recall. This suggests the TFT is more capable of detecting actual shifts in data structure rather than just following the most common state.


## Conclusion 
While the LSTM appeared superior on paper, the Temporal Fusion Transformer demonstrated a unique potential for identifying complex structural changes in market behavior. Future research will explore larger datasets and refined transformer architectures like the "Informer" to further enhance regime classification.
